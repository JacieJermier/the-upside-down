About This Visualization

This visualization explores how wastewater surveillance detected COVID-19 outbreaks before hospitals saw the surge. Using data from the CDC's National Wastewater Surveillance System (NWSS) and clinical case reports, it reveals the lead time between underground detection and surface-level outbreaks.

The Metaphor

Inspired by Stranger Things, the visualization frames wastewater data as the Upside Down — a hidden layer of reality where threats are detectable before they break through to the surface. This isn't just decoration: Byrne et al. (2019) showed that figurative frames in visualization expand expressiveness by layering descriptive meaning onto abstract data.

How to Read It

Upside Down (mirrored map): Red dots show wastewater sampling sites detecting viral RNA. Brighter = stronger signal.

The Breach (flip): When clinical cases surge, the map flips to normal orientation. Cracks appear where the virus broke through to hospitals.

Ridgeline plot: Shows wastewater signal (solid) vs clinical cases (dashed) per region. The horizontal gap between peaks is the lead time.
